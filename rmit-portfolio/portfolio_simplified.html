I came to design through engineering. A Biomedical Engineering diploma taught me to build sensing devices around clinical needs; studying Computer Science and Design at SUTD taught me to start with people and systems before the device. The problems I enjoy sit where people, infrastructure and technology meet: a canteen, a bus stop, a rehabilitation clinic, a concert hall.

My process moves from observation and data to working prototypes in code, sensors and 3D. Since 2019 I have also lit and filmed live performance, which taught me to design for atmosphere and timing as well as function.

At RMIT I want to push both halves of that practice further: the theory of designing for sustainment and sociotechnical change in GRAP1042, and creative-coding prototypes that respond to real-time media in ARCH1477.

Auraplane

Communal dining, undisturbed — a seating system for SUTD's open-air canteen

Year
2025
Context
SUTD 3.007 Design Thinking & Innovation
Team
Group project — SC10 G8 "Snack Overflow"
My role
Group leader
Methods
Site mapping · AEIOU · Real-Win-Worth · contextualisation drawing · 3D rendering · LDR prototype

BriefAt lunch, staff in SUTD's canteen hunt for seats, guard their bags, give up privacy and fend off birds raiding unattended trays. How might the canteen offer more than queuing and seat-finding?

ProcessWe read the canteen as a system rather than a room: crowd heat-maps, traffic flow, queue lines, temperature zones, wind, noise and light levels. AEIOU mapping and Real-Win-Worth scoring narrowed the concepts, and contextualisation drawings resolved storage, magnetic placement and modular joints.

OutcomeA clip-on partition that hangs behind the chair, locates on embedded table magnets and switches between frosted privacy and a clear "discussion board". An enclosed light-dependent-resistor sensor toggles the modes without contact, keeping electronics food-safe. Reflective edges deter birds passively, and the system is designed to run without an external power source.

Which cup is greener?

A life-cycle analysis of single-use, "biodegradable" and reusable cups

Year
2025
Context
SUTD coursework — life-cycle analysis report
Team
Group project
My role
Group leader
Methods
Functional-unit definition · embodied-energy inventory (material, manufacture, use, end-of-life, recycling credit) · Granta EduPack data

BriefCafés swap plastic cups for "eco" alternatives on the strength of a label. Does the label survive a look at the cup's whole life?

ProcessWe defined a functional unit for a café serving 219,000 cups a year, then traced the embodied energy of a 10 g PET cup, a 105 g solid-bamboo cup and a 100 g polycarbonate reusable cup: raw material and processing, moulding or machining, washing in use, incineration at end of life, and credits for recycling (18.2%, 0% and 0.7% recycle fractions).

OutcomePer cup, PET embodies 1.12 MJ, bamboo 5.91 MJ and polycarbonate 12.70 MJ. On these figures the reusable cup only overtakes PET after about a dozen uses, and the "biodegradable" bamboo cup costs about five times the energy of plastic because it is ten times heavier and is incinerated. Impact is decided by use patterns and end-of-life systems, not by material labels.

Relevance to RMIT
GRAP1042Product life-cycle and whole-of-systems thinking; questioning assumed notions of use, utility and consumption.

Off-grid Smart Bin

Sizing a standalone solar power system for smart street furniture

Year
2025
Context
SUTD 10.023 Designing Energy Systems
Team
Group project — G3-8
My role
Group leader
Methods
Shunt-resistor & oscilloscope load testing · battery and PV sizing · peak-sun-hour analysis · CAD casing · prototyping

BriefSingapore's Smart Nation 2.0 puts sensors, motors and radios into public bins, and each one becomes another load on the grid. Could a smart bin power itself?

ProcessWe measured the bin's current in each state with a 1.2 Ω shunt resistor and an oscilloscope: start-up 168 mA, idle 11 mA, opening 218 mA, closing 235 mA. At 1,437 open–close cycles a day the true load is 420.5 mAh. We sized the system against a worst-case December figure of 3.97 peak sun hours and tested charging in six lighting conditions, from full sun to indoor light.

OutcomeA 3.7 V Li-Po battery of at least 1,000 mAh (867 mAh required), a 6 V, 480 mA panel array and a charge controller, in a side-hook casing. Mounting the panel on the side keeps its weight off the lid mechanism and leaves the wiring undisturbed when the lid is removed. The report closes with an upscaled design for an outdoor bin.

Relevance to RMIT
GRAP1042Energy within a sociotechnical system of users, infrastructure and policy; design strategies for sustainment.
ARCH1477Prototyping driven by measured, real-world data.

Predicting Transport CO2

A transparent data model for testing mode-shift strategies

Year
2025
Context
SUTD Data Driven World
Team
Group project — Group 3
My role
Group leader
Tools
Python · pandas · data cleaning · pair plots · correlation matrix · linear regression (gradient descent)

BriefTransport planners need numbers to justify funding and to meet 2030 and 2035 targets, but they lack a single, transparent tool that links mode mix to CO2. How might annual passenger-traffic data predict transport emissions and let planners test mode-shift scenarios?

ProcessWe assembled a 33-year U.S. dataset of scale indicators (registered vehicles, population), urban development (rapid-transit-to-resident ratio) and passenger traffic by transit, highway, intercity rail and aviation. After cleaning and merging, pair plots and a correlation matrix checked linearity and collinearity, and the model went through three iterations with log-transformed features.

OutcomeR² rose from 0.738 to 0.852 and MAE fell from 41.1 to 31.7. A simple input interface lets a user change each traffic mode and read the predicted emissions, so the model works as a what-if tool rather than a black box.

Relevance to RMIT
GRAP1042Macro-level environmental concerns; mobility as a pattern of consumption shaped by infrastructure.
ARCH1477Code that manipulates data behind a usable interface.

Eudaimonia at the Bus Stop

Participatory design for a more inclusive, less anxious wait

Year
2026
Context
SUTD 02.005 Introduction to Human-Centred Design
Team
Group project
My role
Group leader
Methods
Ethnographic observation · interviews · participatory workshops · affinity mapping · Real-Win-Worth · Unity simulation · user testing

BriefSingapore's bus stops work, but waiting at them is full of small failures: uncertain arrivals, missed buses, hard boarding for less mobile riders, and missed signals between commuters and bus captains.

ProcessObservation and interviews with teenagers, elderly commuters and a bus captain fed participatory workshops and affinity mapping. Ideas were scored with Real-Win-Worth against five definitions of success: reliability, accessibility, comfort, clarity and flow.

OutcomeA combined system: a press-to-register panel that lights up and vibrates when your bus arrives, a clearer arrival display showing bus order, floor LED flaggers and bollards that light for the arriving bus, and more inclusive space for impaired commuters. We built it as a walk-through Unity simulation and tested it with diverse users before iterating.

心间 Close to Heart

A speech-first companion for China's empty-nest elderly

Year
August 2025 — one-week immersion
Context
SUTD GEO programme × Chongqing University (DIVE)
Team
Cross-cultural group of five
My role
Group leader
Methods
User insights · HMW · ideation · precedent study · GenAI speech pipeline · wireframes · business model

BriefMany elderly parents in China live alone. They are lonely but do not want to burden their children, and complex apps, password fatigue and fear of scams keep them away from digital help. How might we provide everyday companionship and timely reassurance, so families feel reassured and the elderly feel accompanied?

ProcessWe mapped insights on companionship, adoption, form factor and privacy, generated ten deliberately wild ideas, benchmarked PARO, ElliQ and a fall-detecting lamp, and converged on a familiar, non-robotic object that is used only by voice.

OutcomeA plush or figurine with a swappable chassis around a microphone, speaker and Wi-Fi/BLE module. Speech flows through speech recognition, then language understanding with a personalised tone planner (dialect, politeness, pacing), then a reply composer and Mandarin text-to-speech. The stack also plans proactive nudges and quiet hours, and a wireframed family status page shows mood across the day.

Relevance to RMIT
GRAP1042Social sustainability; designing within family, cultural and digital systems rather than around them.

Interactive IADL Assessment Device

Turning a rehabilitation task into measurable motion and force data

Year
2021
Context
Temasek Polytechnic — Biomedical Engineering major project
Team
Team of three
My role
Group leader and Software Lead
Tools
Python · OpenCV · Pygame · Tkinter · SQLite3 · Matplotlib · force-sensitive resistors · HC-05 Bluetooth

BriefTherapists usually judge Instrumental Activities of Daily Living, such as wiping a table, by eye. Progress between sessions is hard to compare, and patients find repetitive tasks dull.

ProcessI led the team and, as Software Lead, built the desktop application: a Tkinter interface with local SQLite patient records, an augmented Pygame wiping game, and OpenCV tracking of coloured markers on the hand, elbow and shoulder that computes joint angles live. A glove of silicone-padded force sensors streams grip force over Bluetooth serial. We also tested electroluminescent markers for more reliable tracking.

OutcomeMotion and force are captured together during an engaging task, with force accuracy of about ±0.5 N and playback for review across sessions. The project supported a patent filing through IP2SG and was presented at ARTSIC.

Relevance to RMIT
ARCH1477Computer vision, real-time sensor input, game logic and a custom interface working as one prototype.

Stalactite

A parametric study of cave formation in Rhino and Grasshopper

Year
2024
Context
SUTD — Computational Design Thinking
Team
Individual project
Tools
Rhino · Grasshopper · GhPython · sine functions · Voronoi tessellation

BriefCave formations are irregular, yet they follow simple physical rules. Could that controlled irregularity be described as a small set of parameters rather than sculpted by hand?

ProcessI scripted the overhang in GhPython. Summed powers of sine functions displace a grid of points in z, and an if-condition flattens everything above a threshold so only the hanging half-cycles remain as stalactites. The base is a Voronoi terrain whose cells are lifted by a curve-mapped displacement, and a Cull Pattern step filters out invalid geometry before the border is closed.

OutcomeA 3 × 3 design matrix that varies overhang complexity, base resolution and terrain variation, plus an annotated definition that can regenerate any variant.

Relevance to RMIT
ARCH1477Parametric and algorithmic modelling: form generated from rules and data rather than drawn.
GhPython — overhang surface (from the definition)
z = sz * pow(height * sin((sx * x + tx) * (sy * y + ty)), 5) + pow(sin(sx * x + tx), 2) + tz
if z > tz:
    z = tz

Points above the threshold are flattened, leaving only the downward half-cycles that read as stalactites.

What I want from GRAP1042

So far I have analysed single products and sites (works 01–06). I want the theory that sits behind that work: how sociotechnical systems are structured, where design can shift patterns of production and consumption, and how to argue a design position in writing as clearly as I can in visuals.

What I want from ARCH1477

I have programmed light on consoles (works 12–13) and written computer-vision and audio tools (07, 11), but never joined the two. I want to learn an open-source creative-coding framework properly and use it to prototype interactions that respond to live sound, movement and camera input.
